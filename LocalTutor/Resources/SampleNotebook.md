# Photosynthesis: Study Notes

## 1. Overview

Photosynthesis is the process by which plants, algae and some bacteria convert light energy into chemical energy stored in sugars. It takes place mainly in the leaves, inside organelles called **chloroplasts**.

The overall balanced equation is:

6 CO₂ + 6 H₂O + light energy → C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6 O₂

Carbon dioxide enters the leaf through pores called **stomata**, water is absorbed by the roots and carried up through the **xylem**, and oxygen is released as a by-product.

## 2. Chloroplast Structure

- **Outer and inner membranes** enclose the chloroplast.
- **Thylakoids** are flattened membrane sacs that contain chlorophyll. Stacks of thylakoids are called **grana** (singular: granum).
- The **stroma** is the fluid surrounding the thylakoids. It contains enzymes, ribosomes and the chloroplast's own DNA.
- **Chlorophyll a** is the primary pigment. **Chlorophyll b** and **carotenoids** are accessory pigments that broaden the range of light absorbed.

Chlorophyll absorbs red (around 680 nm) and blue (around 430 nm) light most strongly and reflects green light, which is why leaves look green.

## 3. The Light-Dependent Reactions

These reactions happen in the **thylakoid membranes** and need light.

1. **Photosystem II** absorbs light, exciting electrons in chlorophyll.
2. Water is split (**photolysis**): 2 H₂O → 4 H⁺ + 4 e⁻ + O₂. This replaces the lost electrons and is the source of the oxygen released.
3. Excited electrons pass along an **electron transport chain**. Their energy pumps H⁺ ions into the thylakoid space.
4. **Photosystem I** re-energizes the electrons, which reduce NADP⁺ to **NADPH**.
5. H⁺ ions flow back into the stroma through **ATP synthase**, producing **ATP**. This is called **chemiosmosis** (photophosphorylation).

Products: ATP, NADPH (both go to the Calvin cycle) and O₂ (released).

## 4. The Calvin Cycle (Light-Independent Reactions)

The Calvin cycle takes place in the **stroma** and uses the ATP and NADPH from the light reactions.

1. **Carbon fixation:** The enzyme **RuBisCO** attaches CO₂ to ribulose bisphosphate (**RuBP**, 5 carbons). The unstable 6-carbon product splits into two molecules of 3-phosphoglycerate (3-PGA).
2. **Reduction:** ATP and NADPH convert 3-PGA into glyceraldehyde-3-phosphate (**G3P**).
3. **Regeneration:** Most G3P is used, with more ATP, to regenerate RuBP so the cycle continues.

For every 3 CO₂ fixed, the cycle produces 1 net G3P and uses 9 ATP and 6 NADPH. Two G3P molecules can be combined to make one glucose.

RuBisCO is thought to be the most abundant protein on Earth. It is slow and can also bind O₂ instead of CO₂, a wasteful process called **photorespiration**.

## 5. Limiting Factors

The rate of photosynthesis is limited by whichever factor is in shortest supply:

- **Light intensity:** rate rises with intensity until another factor becomes limiting.
- **CO₂ concentration:** more CO₂ increases the rate up to a plateau.
- **Temperature:** enzymes such as RuBisCO work faster as temperature rises, but denature above roughly 40 °C.
- **Water availability:** water stress causes stomata to close, cutting off CO₂.

## 6. C4 and CAM Plants

Some plants have adaptations that reduce photorespiration in hot, dry conditions.

| Pathway | Example plants | Key adaptation |
|---|---|---|
| C3 | Wheat, rice, oak | Standard Calvin cycle; most plants |
| C4 | Maize, sugarcane | CO₂ is first fixed into a 4-carbon compound in mesophyll cells, then released to RuBisCO in bundle-sheath cells |
| CAM | Cacti, pineapple | Stomata open at night to take in CO₂, which is stored as acids and used during the day |

## 7. Key Terms

- **Autotroph:** an organism that makes its own food.
- **Photolysis:** splitting of water by light energy.
- **Chemiosmosis:** ATP production driven by an H⁺ gradient.
- **Carbon fixation:** incorporating inorganic CO₂ into organic molecules.
- **Compensation point:** light intensity at which photosynthesis equals respiration.
